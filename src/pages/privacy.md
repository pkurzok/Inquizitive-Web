# Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 October 2026

Inquizitive turns any topic into a quiz. It has no accounts, no login and no server of my own. It contains no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking. The free version shows ads from Kickstart Exchange, described below; it is the only third-party service the app sends data to on its own.

This policy explains exactly what leaves your device, where it goes, and why.

## Apple Intelligence: Private Cloud Compute and on-device

Quizzes are written by Apple Intelligence. The topic you type, your answers to the refinement questions, and the quiz being written are processed by Apple’s **Private Cloud Compute** when it is available, and otherwise by the language model **on your device**.

Private Cloud Compute is operated by Apple. Apple states that data sent to it is used only to fulfil your request, is not retained, and is not accessible to Apple or to me. See [Apple’s Private Cloud Compute overview](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/) and [Apple’s privacy policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). The on-device model sends nothing anywhere.

## Research with Wikipedia, Wiktionary and Wikidata

When Private Cloud Compute writes a quiz, it can look facts up so that the questions are correct and can show their sources. Your device then sends search requests to **Wikipedia, Wiktionary and Wikidata**, operated by the Wikimedia Foundation, Inc., 1 Montgomery Street, Suite 1600, San Francisco, CA 94104, USA.

Each request contains only the search term the model chose (for example “Punic Wars”), the language, and an app identifier (`Inquizitive/` followed by the app version). It contains no account, no device identifier and nothing else about you. As with any web request, the Wikimedia servers see your device’s IP address. The Wikimedia Foundation’s [privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy) applies to these requests.

When you tap a source link in a quiz, the article opens in your browser, and the privacy policy of that website applies.

## Ads in the free version: Kickstart Exchange

The free version shows banners for other indie apps from **Kickstart Exchange**, operated by Hudson Heavy Industries Ltd., 37 Great Pulteney Street, Bath, BA2 4DA, United Kingdom.

To load and count banners, the app sends Kickstart Exchange app-level information only: the app’s identifier, platform, app and SDK version, the App Store storefront country, a signed App Store transaction record that Kickstart Exchange verifies and then discards, and whether a banner was shown or tapped. It sends no account, no device or advertising identifier, and the app never asks for permission to track you. Kickstart Exchange counts views and taps per app, not per person or device. Its network provider Cloudflare sees your IP address, as with any web request; Kickstart Exchange states that it doesn’t store it. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (financing the free version). See the [Kickstart Exchange privacy policy](https://exchange.kickstart.tools/privacy).

With Pro, no banners are loaded and nothing is sent to Kickstart Exchange.

## Your quizzes and iCloud

Your quizzes, scores and progress are stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, they sync between your devices through your **private iCloud database** (Apple CloudKit). That storage belongs to your Apple Account: I have no access to it and receive no copy. If you don’t use iCloud, your quizzes stay on the device. Deleting the app, or deleting a quiz, removes it. A quiz you share is the exception, described below.

## Sharing a quiz

When you share a quiz, the app encrypts it on your device and stores the encrypted copy in the app’s **public iCloud database** (Apple CloudKit), so that the person you send the link to can load it without an account.

The key is part of the link. It is not stored with the shared copy and never reaches this website, so I cannot read a shared quiz; only people who have the link can open it. The key stays with the quiz on your devices and, if you use iCloud, in your private iCloud database.

The shared copy holds the quiz: its title, topic, questions, answers, explanations, sources, symbol and colour. It holds no name, no score and no progress. A score travels only inside the link you send.

The copy can already be uploaded when the share sheet opens, even if you then cancel. As with any CloudKit record, Apple stores which iCloud account created it; the app never reads that.

The copy stays until you delete the quiz. Deleting the quiz deletes the copy, and links you have sent stop working. Quizzes that others have already added stay with them.

Opening a link loads the encrypted copy from Apple and needs no account. The page a link shows in a browser is part of this static website and never receives the part of the link after `#`.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

## Pro, tips and in-app purchases

Pro and the optional tips in Settings are processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. I receive no payment details and no personal information about you, only anonymous sales totals from Apple.

## No tracking

- no analytics, no usage statistics
- no crash reporting
- no advertising identifier and no request for permission to track
- no accounts, no email sign-up
- no third-party SDK that sends data anywhere, apart from Kickstart Exchange in the free version

## This website

This website is a static site. It sets no cookies and uses no analytics. The hosting provider processes technical connection data (such as your IP address and the requested page) to deliver the page and keep the service secure. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

## Contacting me

If you write to [inquizitive@peterkurzok.de](mailto:inquizitive@peterkurzok.de), your message and the data it contains are stored so that I can answer it. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where your request concerns a contract, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. I keep the correspondence as long as needed to deal with your enquiry, and longer only where the law requires it.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), and to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, normally the data protection authority of the German state in which I am established.
